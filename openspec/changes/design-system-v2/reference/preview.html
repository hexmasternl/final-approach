Diagnosis

Why the current system reads as primitive

It is not a lack of assets. Four rules in the current system produce the look, and each has a direct replacement.

Shapes are built from primitives. The tree is five circles, paler than the lawn it stands on. Proposed: a lobed canopy in three tones, a trunk, and a shadow that plants it on the ground.

Strict top-down hides what things are. A house seen from straight above is a roof diagram. Proposed: standing things lean slightly, so the south wall, door and windows show.

One light, nowhere. Nothing on the ground has a lit side, a shaded side or a shadow, so nothing has volume. Proposed: one sun, top-left, for every object.

Every inner line is navy. Details drawn in the outline colour turn into noise at small sizes. Proposed: navy for the silhouette only, inner lines in a darker tone of the fill.

The outline does the work of depth. A 4dp outline plus a 5dp navy lip makes a quarter of the button dark edge. Proposed: a 2.5dp outline, and a lip in the button's own colour.

World · principles

Nine rules for everything on the ground

Each rule is checkable on a single sprite. Together they replace "flat and bright" and the four-fill budget.

  1. W1One sun, top-leftEvery object is lit from the top-left: a light tone on the north-west side, a shade tone on the south-east, a hard shadow cast down-right.
  2. W2Three tones per materialLight, base and shade, as flat cel shapes. Never a gradient. A sprite uses at most three materials.
  3. W3Navy outside, tinted insideThe silhouette is outline. Every line inside it is the material's darkest tone, so detail adds form without adding noise.
  4. W4Line weight is rankAircraft 4, landing zones 3, scenery 2.5, inner detail 1.25. The eye goes to the thickest line first, and that is always an aircraft.
  5. W5Standing things show a south wallAnything with height leans a little towards the camera: roof on top, wall below. Three height tiers set the wall and the shadow length.
  6. W6Flat ground has no outlineRoads, fields, water and aprons are edged in their own darker tone. Only things with height, and landing zones, earn a navy line.
  7. W7Edges are transitionsTwo ground materials never meet on a bare line. A runway has a shoulder and rough grass, water has shallows and sand, a road has a verge.
  8. W8Detail clusters, open ground restsTufts, flowers and props gather around objects and edges. The mown airfield and the approach corridors stay calm.
  9. W9Aircraft own the top of the rangeThe brightest white and the most saturated colours belong to aircraft and paths. Scenery whites are cream, scenery colours are a step muted.

World · kit

Scenery, redrawn

Shown twice their world size. Every sprite follows W1 to W9; trees and pines come in sizes, cars in muted colours.

Landing zones and ground, in place

Runway 09. Shoulder and rough-grass fringe, threshold bars, aiming blocks, touchdown marks, tyre rubber, edge lights, green threshold lights and a four-box approach indicator. The zone still reads first: it is the only ground shape with a navy line.
Helipad and taxiway. A concrete pad with slab joints and corner lights, the yellow ring and H as today. Taxiways are a lighter asphalt with a yellow centre line, so they never look like a place to land.
Apron. Tower, terminal and hangar stand on their south walls. Parked aircraft keep the current sprite in grey. Slab joints and stand marks are tinted, not navy.
Countryside. Unmown meadow with patches, a pond with shallows, sand, reeds and ducks, and woods whose trees overlap north to south. Mowing stripes exist only inside the airfield.

Night, from the same sprites

Night redefines the tones and nothing else. Windows switch to window light, shadows deepen, and aircraft and UI stay bright as they do today.

UI · principles

Seven rules for controls and panels

  1. U1Moulded, not boxedA control is a face, a highlight along its top edge and a lip in its own 700 tone, wrapped by one thin outline.
  2. U2A lip means pressableButtons, cards and toggles have a lip and sink when pressed. Readouts, chips and tags are flat. Nothing else may look like a button.
  3. U3Thin line, thick formThe outline is 2.5dp everywhere in the UI. Depth comes from the lip and a flat contact shadow, not from a heavier edge.
  4. U4Tinted insideDividers, keylines and chip edges take a darker tone of their surface. Navy is for the outer edge and for text.
  5. U5Nested radiiAn inner corner is the outer radius minus the gap between them, so shapes sit inside each other without pinched corners.
  6. U6Tower paperworkSurfaces borrow from the control tower: HUD readouts are flight strips, dialogs are boarding passes with a perforated stub, verdicts are rubber stamps.
  7. U7Disabled is flat, not fadedA disabled control loses its colour, lip and shine and keeps full opacity, so it stays legible on grass and sky.

Four calls that are yours to overrule

  • Leaning top-down for standing sceneryBuildings, trees and the tower show a south wall and never rotate. Things that move or turn (aircraft, vehicles, animals, boats) stay strictly top-down. It is the biggest single gain in recognisability, and the biggest break with today's rule.
  • Nunito replaces Inconsolata for body textMonospace paragraphs are a large part of the coarse feel. Inconsolata keeps the tower voice where it earns it: labels, readouts, callsigns.
  • Readouts lose their lipScore, lives and next-up become flat strips. The HUD gets lighter, and "a lip means pressable" becomes a rule players can trust.
  • No navy outline on flat groundRoads, fields and ponds are edged in their own tone. This is what lets runways and aircraft stand out against a much richer field.

After approval

The design system comes first, as one OpenSpec change: tokens.json with the ramps and ranks, the brand book and world rules rewritten around W1 to W9 and U1 to U7, every scenery SVG redrawn, the component guidelines and showcase.html regenerated, and the published design system updated. Porting it to the Compose theme and sprites is a second change. Island and Savanna get the same treatment as Meadow Field here.
