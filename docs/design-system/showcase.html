FinalApproach

The design system, version 2, for a top-down air traffic game that looks like a box of toys tipped onto a lawn.

FoundationsPrinciples

1Toy scaleSizes are exaggerated, not realistic. A jumbo is nearly twice a light prop.
2Silhouette before colourTypes, zones and states are told apart by shape first. Colour confirms.
3The field stays clearUI lives in flat strips at the edges. Nothing covers a landing zone.

World: nine rules for everything on the ground

W1One sun, top-leftEvery object is lit from the top-left: a lit tone to the north-west, a shade tone to the south-east, a hard shadow cast down-right.
W2Three tones per materialLight, base and shade, as flat shapes. Never a gradient. A sprite uses at most three materials.
W3Navy outside, tinted insideThe silhouette is outline. Every line inside it is the material’s darkest tone.
W4Line weight is rankAircraft 4, landing zones 3, scenery 2.5, inner detail 1.25. The thickest line is always an aircraft.
W5Standing things show a south wallBuildings, trees and the tower lean a little: roof on top, wall below, in one of three height tiers. They never rotate.
W6Flat ground has no outlineRoads, fields, water and aprons are edged in their own darker tone. Only things with height, and landing zones, earn a navy line.
W7Edges are transitionsA runway has a shoulder and rough grass, water has shallows and sand, a road has a kerb and a verge.
W8Detail clusters, open ground restsTufts, flowers and props gather around objects and edges. Only the airfield is mown in stripes.
W9Aircraft own the top of the rangeThe brightest white and the most saturated colours belong to aircraft and paths. Scenery whites are cream.

UI: seven rules for controls and panels

U1Moulded, not boxedA control is a face, a highlight along its top edge and a lip in its own 700 tone, wrapped by one thin outline.
U2A lip means pressableButtons, cards, the toggle knob and the selected segment have a lip. Readouts, chips and stickers are flat.
U3Thin line, thick formThe UI outline is 2.5dp. Depth comes from the lip and a flat contact shadow.
U4Tinted insideDividers, keylines and chip edges take a darker tone of their surface. Navy is for the outer edge and for text.
U5Nested radiiAn inner corner is the outer radius minus the gap between them.
U6Tower paperworkHUD readouts are flight strips, dialogs are boarding passes with a perforated stub, verdicts are stamps.
U7Disabled is flat, not fadedA disabled control loses its colour, lip and shine and keeps full opacity.

FoundationsColour

149 colour tokens in three themes: Day, Night and Dawn. Materials and accents are ramps with fixed roles: 300 is the lit tone, 500 the base, 700 the shade or the lip, 900 the tinted inner line, 100 and 200 a highlight. Swatches follow the theme switch; values list Day first.

A 4px spacing grid, five line ranks, nested radii, and depth from a lip and a flat contact shadow. Nothing in the UI is blurred.

AssetsScenery

Everything on the ground that is not a landing zone, drawn on the nine world rules. Standing things show a south wall and never rotate; things that move are strictly top-down. Each sprite carries a height tier and the silhouette of its cast shadow.

  • Silhouette in outline at line-ui; inner lines in the material's 700 or 900 step at line-detail. An aircraft keeps line-hero.
  • At most three materials in one sprite, each in flat tones of its ramp. Glass, lamps and small trims do not count as a material.
  • Never fuselage, a path colour or sun-500. Whites are wall-100 or hide-100. Only the tower cab (cherry) and the windsock (tangerine) keep an accent.
  • Height tiers: S throws its shadow 3 by 4 units, M 12 by 14, L 11 by 13 from a raised crown. All hard shadows share one layer in shadow-cast at opacity-shadow-cast.
  • Windows take window: glass by day, a warm light in Night and Dawn.
  • Scenery never covers a runway, a helipad or a landing zone, and only flat ground lies under the HUD band.
